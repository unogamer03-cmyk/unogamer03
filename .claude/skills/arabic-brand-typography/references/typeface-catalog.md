# Arabic Typeface Catalog

A working shortlist, organized by script model and register. It is not exhaustive and catalogs change: always tell the user to confirm the current weights, Latin companion, and license on the foundry or distributor site before purchase or production. If you suggest a face that isn't listed here, say how confident you are that it exists and what its license is.

License shorthand: **OFL** = SIL Open Font License (free for commercial use, including logos; can't sell the font itself). **Commercial** = paid EULA; desktop, web, app, and broadcast are often licensed separately.

## 6. Overused choices and fresher alternatives

Cairo, Tajawal, Almarai, Noto, and DIN Next Arabic are everywhere in Saudi digital and corporate work. They're fine for UI and body text, but they make an identity look like everyone else's. For a brand's *display* and logotype voice, prefer:
- a commercial face with a distinctive Arabic design (29LT, Commercial Type, Typotheque, Rosetta), or
- a libre face as a starting point for a **custom-drawn** logotype (Reem Kufi, Qahiri, Kufam, El Messiri), or
- fully custom lettering.

Keep the common faces for UI and body text, where familiarity helps.

## 7. Selection notes

- **Persian/Urdu-origin designs.** Check that ك, ي, ه and numerals follow Arabic conventions for Gulf readers. Persian forms (ک, ی, Persian digits ۴۵۶) read as foreign in KSA.
- **Weight range.** A brand system usually needs at least Regular and Bold Arabic, ideally Light through Black. Some libre faces have only 1–2 weights.
- **Diacritics (تشكيل).** If the brand uses vocalized text (children's, educational, religious-adjacent, poetry), test the face's harakat positioning.
- **Variable fonts.** These help match Arabic weight to Latin precisely. Confirm the variable file is licensed for the intended medium.
